# Origin and Stewardship

## Origin and technical lineage

Alignment Governance Stack (AGS) was originated and developed by Michael Bower as part of the broader Human Agency Infrastructure research program. This attribution identifies the project's human origin and technical lineage; it does not claim ownership of general concepts such as governance, runtime governance, human agency, authorization, receipts, or independent validation.

The [canonical repository](https://github.com/mnbower-research/alignment-governance-stack) and its Git history are the authoritative technical lineage, beginning with the initial scaffold (`e7963e0`, authored by Michael Bower). The [release history](docs/RELEASE_HISTORY.md) and [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) describe its development milestones. Attribution to the originator does not erase subsequent contributors or their contributions.

## Stewardship philosophy

**Claim lineage, not control.**

Attribution preserves historical and technical provenance. AGS is intended to remain openly inspectable, independently implementable, challengeable, and extensible. Disagreement, critiques, forks, and competing implementations are legitimate. No originator should become an unchallengeable governing authority merely by having originated the framework.

The AGS name identifies the originating framework and its canonical lineage. The underlying general principles of governance are not claimed as proprietary concepts. Implementations, extensions, critiques, and compatible systems remain governed by the repository's applicable license where it applies and should preserve appropriate attribution where required.

The repository uses [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE). This statement does not change that license, add permission requirements for general governance mechanisms, or impose the repository's license on unrelated independent work. [Citation metadata](CITATION.cff) provides a way to acknowledge AGS in research and engineering work.

## Governance of governance

**The governor must also be governed.**

Stewardship of AGS should itself remain transparent, reviewable, and subject to challenge. Changes to the architecture, terminology, implementation, and stewardship should preserve visible rationale and technical history. Canonical status identifies the project's maintained reference; it does not make its claims immune to evidence, criticism, or revision.

## Canonical, compatible, and derivative work

- **Canonical/official AGS:** the originating repository linked above and its maintained specifications, including the [canonical Modular Architecture](docs/MODULAR_ARCHITECTURE.md). Official status denotes project lineage, not independent assurance or deployment certification.
- **Independent/compatible implementations:** separately maintained systems that implement AGS interfaces or governance functions. Compatibility claims should identify the specification revision, supported boundaries, evidence, and limitations; they do not by themselves imply official endorsement.
- **Derivative research inspired by AGS:** work that acknowledges the lineage while developing, challenging, or departing from its architecture. Such work need not claim compatibility or agreement.

These distinctions clarify provenance. They do not reserve general governance ideas to Michael Bower or require his permission to use those ideas.
